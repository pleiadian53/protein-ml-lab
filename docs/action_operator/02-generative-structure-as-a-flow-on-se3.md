# Generative Structure as a Flow on $SE(3)$

> **Status: 🌱 new — conceptual.** This chapter is study, not yet a from-scratch artifact: it re-reads RFdiffusion / FrameDiff / FrameFlow through the action-operator lens, building on the verified frame machinery of [Phase 2](../alphafold2/README.md). A from-scratch $\mathrm{IGSO}(3)$ sampler plus a toy $SE(3)$ flow integrator is the natural next code artifact. Prerequisite: [Residue frames as $SE(3)$ operators](01-residue-frames-as-se3-operators.md).

[Chapter 1](01-residue-frames-as-se3-operators.md) gave the *deterministic* operator picture: a residue frame is an $SE(3)$ operator, and AlphaFold refines a structure by rolling such operators forward toward a single answer. This chapter makes it *generative*. Instead of refining toward one structure, we **grow a structure from noise by integrating a flow on the group of frames** — and this turns out to be the exact protein twin of ssl-lab's conditioned latent rollout.

## The whole idea in four lines

1. Put the structure's $L$ frames at pure noise, $\{T_i(0)\}$, sampled from a simple prior *on the group*.
2. Learn a **vector field** $v_\theta$ that points, at each frame and each time $\tau$, toward data.
3. **Integrate** it: $\dfrac{dT_i}{d\tau} = v_\theta(\{T_j\}, \tau)$, carrying $T_i(0) \to T_i(1)$. The endpoint $\{T_i(1)\}$ is a generated structure.
4. Build $v_\theta$ to be $SE(3)$-equivariant, and the whole generative map respects the symmetry by construction.

Read line 3 again: it is a **rollout of operators**, exactly as in [Chapter 1](01-residue-frames-as-se3-operators.md) and in ssl-lab's [temporal world model](https://pleiadian53.github.io/ssl-lab/operator_world_models/02-jepa-as-a-temporal-world-model/) — only the "time" axis is now *denoising time* $\tau$ rather than physical time, and the state is the tuple of frames rather than a behavioral latent.

## The prior is a *distribution over operators*: $\mathrm{IGSO}(3) \times \mathcal{N}$

To start from noise you need a distribution over rigid motions — a point in $\Delta(SE(3))$, the **space of distributions over the operator parameters**. The deployed construction (FrameDiff, RFdiffusion, FrameFlow) is

$$
\Delta(SE(3))  =  \underbrace{\mathrm{IGSO}(3)}_{\text{rotations}} \times \underbrace{\mathcal{N}(\mathbb{R}^3)}_{\text{translations}},
$$

an **isotropic Gaussian on the rotation group** ($\mathrm{IGSO}(3)$, the heat kernel on $SO(3)$) times an ordinary Gaussian on translations. This is precisely ssl-lab's policy codomain $\pi_\psi : \mathcal{Z} \to \Delta(\Theta)$ with $\Theta = SE(3)$ — the network emits not one operator but a *distribution* over them, and you sample. The behavioral pole reaches for a Gaussian or mixture over a flat $\Theta$; the protein pole reaches for $\mathrm{IGSO}(3) \times \mathcal{N}$ over the curved group $SE(3)$. Same object, transported onto a manifold.

## "Traveling in denoising-time unfurls 3D space"

Here is the picture worth keeping. Each frame traces a path through $SE(3)$ as $\tau$ runs from noise to data:

```
τ=0 (noise)                                  τ=1 (structure)
 {T_i(0)} ── v_θ ──▶ {T_i(τ₁)} ── v_θ ──▶ ⋯ ──▶ {T_i(1)}
 IGSO(3)×N                                     a folded protein
```

The **trajectory through $SE(3)^L$, generated by integrating the flow, *is* the structure being built**. Nothing places the atoms in one shot; the structure is *unfurled* by following the operator's flow. That is the same sentence as ssl-lab's world-model rollout — "a one-step operator, iterated, is a world model" — read on the rotation-translation group. The deterministic refinement of [Chapter 1](01-residue-frames-as-se3-operators.md) and the generative flow here are two faces of one thing: **operators integrated along an axis**.

## The same $\exp$-of-a-generator move

The flow lives on the group, and you take steps *in the algebra*: an increment of the vector field is an $\mathfrak{se}(3)$ element, exponentiated and composed onto the current frame, $T_i \leftarrow T_i \circ \exp(\mathrm{d}\tau  v_i)$. This is the identical mechanism to ssl-lab's $f_\theta = \exp(M_\theta)$, with one substitution: $M_\theta$ is a *free* learned matrix on the behavioral pole (any near-identity flow), and a *constrained* $\mathfrak{se}(3)$ element here (a rigid flow). The generator algebra is the only thing that changed between a model of a person's week and a model of a protein's fold.

## Equivariance, still demanded

The reason this is the clean pole, again: if the learned vector field $v_\theta$ is $SE(3)$-**equivariant** — rotate-and-translate the input frames and the predicted field rotates-and-translates the same way — then integrating it carries an $SE(3)$-invariant prior to an $SE(3)$-invariant data distribution. The generated *distribution over structures* is therefore symmetry-respecting by construction: the model cannot prefer one orientation of a fold over its rotations, because the physics does not. This is the [Chapter 1](01-residue-frames-as-se3-operators.md) equivariance argument lifted from a single map to a whole flow — and, as there, it is *demanded*, not learned.

## Conditioning — the action-operator payoff, in proteins

ssl-lab's [Part 3](https://pleiadian53.github.io/ssl-lab/operator_world_models/) made the operator *action-conditioned*: feed a context $c_t$ and the operator says *what acted*, enabling counterfactual rollout. The protein twin is **conditional generation**: condition the flow on a motif to scaffold, a symmetry to enforce, or a binding target, and you steer the trajectory toward structures that satisfy it — RFdiffusion's headline capability. "Generate a binder for this target" is, in the framework's terms, *rolling the conditioned operator forward from noise*. The honest caveat transfers too: conditioning shapes the *distribution* the flow lands in; whether a generated structure is physically realizable is a separate, experimentally-grounded question — the protein analogue of ssl-lab's associational-vs-causal line.

## Where this leaves the two poles

Across both chapters the claim has held: protein structure modeling *is* action-operator modeling, evaluated at the **given** pole. Frames are $SE(3)$ operators (Chapter 1); generation integrates a flow of them from a distribution over the group (this chapter); equivariance is demanded throughout. Set beside ssl-lab's **learned** pole — operators on a behavioral latent, structure discovered from data, equivariance learned — the two are one framework with the generator algebra swapped:

| | ssl-lab (learned pole) | protein-ml-lab (given pole) |
|---|---|---|
| state | behavioral latent $z$ | residue frames $\{T_i\} \in SE(3)^L$ |
| operator | $f_\theta = \exp(M_\theta)$, $M$ free | rigid motion, $M \in \mathfrak{se}(3)$ |
| equivariance | **learned** (a loss) | **demanded** (by construction) |
| the "axis" of rollout | physical time $t$ | denoising time $\tau$ |
| distribution over operators | Gaussian / mixture on $\mathbb{R}^m$ | $\mathrm{IGSO}(3) \times \mathcal{N}$ on $SE(3)$ |
